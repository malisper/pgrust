# Verification coverage — what the proofs, the fuzzers, and the regress suite actually execute

This tree measures, at line granularity over the SHIPPED source under
`crates/`, which lines are exercised by each of three verification
programs:

1. **Kani proof coverage** (`kani`) — the per-commit tier of the
   equivalence-proof suite (`proofs/SUITE.tsv`), re-run with
   `--coverage -Z source-coverage` (Kani RFC-0011 source coverage).
2. **Differential fuzz coverage** (`fuzz`) — the three differential
   cargo-fuzz targets (`float_in_diff`, `float_out_diff`, `geo_diff`)
   replayed over their checked-in corpora with `cargo fuzz coverage`.
3. **Regress suite coverage** (`regress`) — the full PostgreSQL
   `parallel_schedule` (230 files) run once against a server built with
   `-Cinstrument-coverage`.

Artifacts (all generated by `proofs/coverage/merge-coverage.py`):

- `proofs/coverage/summary.json` — machine-readable summary (schema below).
- `proofs/coverage/verification-coverage.tsv` — per-file / per-crate / total rows.
- `proofs/coverage/files/<slug>.json` — per-file line detail for the viewer.
- `proofs/coverage/census.json` — expected / ran / walled / failed-to-run
  roll-up for the Kani axis. **No census, no numbers**: see
  "Fail-closed census" below.

## READ THIS FIRST — what these numbers do and do NOT mean

**A covered line is not a verified line.**

- A line "covered by Kani" means: some proof harness, exploring its
  **fenced** input domain (the bounds recorded in the ledger row —
  e.g. `nd≤2, |weight|≤2, dscale≤6`), can reach that line. Within that
  domain the exploration is exhaustive — but the fence is the whole
  point. Coverage says *reachable under the fence*, not *proved for all
  inputs*, and certainly not *proved correct*: correctness is the
  equivalence assertion of the harness, which is a separate claim from
  which lines got touched on the way.
- A line "covered by fuzz" means: at least one corpus input executed it
  once. The differential oracle checked that execution against verbatim
  C — nothing more.
- A line "covered by regress" means: one fixed corpus (the pg_regress
  schedule) executed it once, and the file-level output matched C's
  expected output. This is the weakest of the three per line — a line
  can be executed and wrong in a way the suite's queries never observe.

The project's own gate-blindness law (2026-07-24) is exactly the failure
mode a percentage invites: a green number read as a stronger claim than
the instrument makes. Do not quote `any%` as "X% of pgrust is verified".
The honest sentence is: "X% of scope SLOC is *executed by at least one
verification instrument*."

Conversely, an uncovered line is not an unverified line **claim-wise**
(a proved function's ledger row still holds over its stated domain even
if a sibling branch is dark) — but the uncovered list is the actionable
map of where no instrument currently reaches.

## SLOC rule (the shared denominator)

A line counts as SLOC iff, after stripping `//` and `/* */` comments, it
contains at least one character other than whitespace and `{}()[];,`.
Blank lines, comment-only lines, attribute-free brace/bracket lines do
not count. `#[cfg(test)]`-gated items, `src/tests.rs` files, and
`tests/` dirs are excluded from the denominator entirely (not shipped
logic). This is a **textual** approximation applied identically to
all three sources (their covered-line sets are intersected with it), so
the three percentages are comparable with each other. It is not a
compiler-grade statement count; do not compare these percentages against
numbers produced with a different rule.

Region-to-line mapping caveats:
- Kani reports source **regions** (spans). Every SLOC line intersecting
  a `COVERED` region is counted — a multi-line region marks all its
  lines, which can slightly overcount relative to LLVM's per-line hits.
- LLVM lcov `DA:` lines with execution count > 0 are counted for fuzz
  and regress.

### Macro-invocation attribution (Kani axis)

Kani reports a region at the span the compiler recorded for the generated
MIR. For a function produced by a `macro_rules!` expansion that span is
inside the macro **definition** body, never the invocation. The tree
declares its fmgr wrappers exactly this way (`fc1!`, `fc1t!`, `fc2!`,
`fc2t!`, `fc_cmp!`, `conv_pair!`, `int_var!`, … — **198 generator macros
across 475 invocation blocks and 2,753 declaration lines tree-wide**), so
without a correction every macro-declared entry point is
**uncoverable by construction**: a real SLOC line in the denominator that
no instrument can ever credit. Measured on adt_float: 54 lines, a
2.44-percentage-point systematic undercount in one crate.

`merge-coverage.py` therefore applies this rule: **a `COVERED` region
inside a `macro_rules!` definition body also credits the source line that
declares the generated function named in that region's `function` field**,
when that name resolves to exactly one declaration site. Implementation
and full rationale: `proofs/coverage/macro_attrib.py`.

Why attribute rather than drop those lines from the denominator (the other
option considered): a `fc*!` invocation line names a real fmgr entry
point, so when no proof reaches it, "uncovered" is the true and useful
answer. In adt_float 67 of the 121 declaration lines are exactly that —
55 wrappers with no harness anywhere, 12 whose only harnesses live in the
calibration/release-gate tiers this capture does not run. Excluding the
block from the denominator would have deleted those 67 honest signals to
fix 54 false ones, and flattered the percentage in the same motion.

The correction cannot over-credit: it matches on generated-function
identity, not on "this macro body has some covered region" (a macro's N
invocations share one definition body, so the latter would credit all N
from one). It is also not a substitute for the fence caveat — a credited
line means that wrapper executed under some harness's fenced domain,
nothing more.

Residual, published not absorbed: a generated function whose name is not
a token at its invocation (name-composing macros) cannot be resolved.
`summary.json.macro_attribution.stats.unresolved` counts those; across
the seven scope crates 262 of 277 declaration lines (94.6%) are uniquely
resolvable, the shortfall being 14 lines in geo (`close2!`, `path_pt!`
style, where the lead identifier is not the generated name) and 1 in
jsonb. `--no-macro-attribution` reproduces the old, biased-low numbers for
comparison. `#[derive]`/proc-macro output is attributed by rustc to the
deriving item's own span, so it needs no correction and gets none.

### Fail-closed census (Kani axis)

A harness that fails to RUN emits no kaniraw, and a pipeline that globs
kaniraw at the end cannot tell that apart from a harness that ran and
covered nothing — the failure reads as legitimately-uncovered code. This
bit for real: under a naive `proofs::` qualification all 20 `bool`
harnesses exited rc=1 with "Failed to match the following harness(es)",
produced zero coverage, and would have merged as 20 harnesses' worth of
silent undercount. Same gate-blindness class as the 96 harnesses that
never ran under `--exact`.

So the Kani axis is fail-closed at three points:

1. `run-kani-coverage.sh` **preflights** every harness name against
   `cargo kani list` for its family (union of the family's `-Z` tokens
   across its SUITE rows) before spending a solve. An unresolvable name is
   recorded `FAILED-TO-RUN` / `NAME-UNRESOLVED` with candidate names. A
   listing that isn't a well-formed kani table degrades to
   `PREFLIGHT-SKIPPED` — never to "no harnesses", which would reject the
   whole family.
2. After each run it enforces a **postcondition**: at least one NEW
   kaniraw whose mangled name ends in that harness's terminal segment.
   rc=0 with no artifact is `FAILED-TO-RUN`, not zero coverage.
3. `merge-coverage.py --census` **refuses to write `summary.json`** (exit
   3, census.json only) if any expected harness produced no coverage,
   unless that harness is listed in `--allow-unmeasured` as
   `family<TAB>harness<TAB>reason` with a non-empty reason. Walls need a
   waiver too — a wall is unmeasured coverage. It prints the explicit
   census (expected / ran / walled / failed-to-run / waived, plus kaniraw
   with no census row) in the same shape as `check-suite-names.py`, and
   `--census` is mandatory: without it the script exits 2 rather than
   quietly publishing a globbed number. `--no-census-required` exists only
   for local exploration and stamps `census_closed: false`; such a run
   must not be published.

## How to regenerate

Base: worktree of the target sha. Kani 0.67.0, cargo-fuzz 0.13.2 +
nightly-2026-07-17 (with `llvm-tools`), rustc 1.96.0 for the server
build, homebrew postgresql@18 for initdb/psql/pg_regress, regress corpus
from `../pgrust-fabled/vendor/postgres-src/src/test/regress`.

### 1. Kani coverage (per family; per-commit tier of SUITE.tsv)

Always through `run-kani-coverage.sh` — it is the only supported runner,
because it is the thing that produces the census the merge step demands:

    proofs/coverage/run-kani-coverage.sh \
        --joblist joblist.tsv --census census.tsv --timeout 900 --log /tmp/cov

Joblist is TSV `family <TAB> suite_harness [<TAB> kani_harness_override]`.
The third column overrides name qualification; it is load-bearing —
`--exact` needs **fully module-qualified** names, several SUITE rows store
unqualified ones, and not every family's harnesses live in `mod proofs`
(`bool`'s live in `mod harnesses`). Preflight will tell you which, by
name, instead of burning 20 solves and reporting zero coverage. The runner
exits nonzero if any job did not end `RAN`.

Per family it is one invocation per harness (flags differ per row; solves
are seconds in the per-commit tier, so build cost dominates once).
Equivalent by hand, for a single harness:

    cd proofs/<family>
    cargo kani <SUITE flags> --coverage -Z source-coverage \
        --exact --harness <qualified-name>

Notes:
- Prefer an **explicit family list per capture, recorded in the joblist**,
  over a wildcard `--kani-glob` — see the provenance correction above; the
  prior capture's float number was right but its log named the wrong
  families.
- Per-family timeouts, not one global: **300 s for the string/jsonb-heavy
  families, 900 s elsewhere** (measured walls were 75-560 s, so 300 s
  loses little and bounds that tail).
- Results accumulate under
  `proofs/<family>/target/kani/<target>/kanicov_<ts>/*kaniraw.json`
  (one JSON per harness, region status COVERED/UNCOVERED, absolute
  source paths — shipped-crate attribution is a path filter on
  `<repo>/crates/`).
- jsonb-probe has **no per-commit tier**; its release-gate cmp cells run
  via `proofs/jsonb-probe/run-cmp.sh <cell> <iters> [nested]` with
  `EXTRA="--coverage -Z source-coverage"` (cell/iters table in
  `run-all.sh`).

### 2. Differential fuzz coverage

    cd fuzz
    # corpora: gen_seeds.sh, or the campaign corpora (session-local;
    # not committed — regenerate or copy before running)
    cargo +nightly-2026-07-17 fuzz coverage <target> corpus/<target>
    $NIGHTLY_SYSROOT/lib/rustlib/<host>/bin/llvm-cov export -format=lcov \
        -instr-profile=coverage/<target>/coverage.profdata \
        target/<host>/coverage/<host>/release/<target> > fuzz-<target>.lcov

(Use the **nightly** toolchain's llvm tools on the fuzz profdata and the
1.96 sysroot tools on the server profdata — profraw formats differ.)

### 3. Regress coverage

    RUSTFLAGS="-Cinstrument-coverage" cargo build -p main_main \
        --bin postgres --profile fast-profile
    PGRUST_BIN=target/fast-profile/postgres NO_BUILD=1 NO_SCORE=1 \
      PGINSTALL=/opt/homebrew/opt/postgresql@18/bin \
      PG_REGRESS=/opt/homebrew/lib/postgresql@18/pgxs/src/test/regress/pg_regress \
      REGRESS_SRC=../pgrust-fabled/vendor/postgres-src/src/test/regress \
      COV_PROFILE_DIR=/tmp/covprof OUTDIR=/tmp/covregress \
      proofs/coverage/pg-regress-cov.sh
    llvm-profdata merge -sparse /tmp/covprof/server-*.profraw -o regress.profdata
    llvm-cov export -format=lcov -instr-profile=regress.profdata \
        target/fast-profile/postgres > regress.lcov

`pg-regress-cov.sh` is `scripts/pg-regress-fast.sh` with exactly two
deltas: the server teardown is graceful SIGTERM-then-wait (the stock
runner `kill -9`s the postmaster, which discards the entire coverage
profile — profiles flush in an atexit handler), and the server env pins
`LLVM_PROFILE_FILE=$COV_PROFILE_DIR/server-%p.profraw` (pgrust is
thread-per-backend: ONE process, one profile; `%p` guards the
crash-resume segments, which each start a fresh postmaster).

### 4. Merge

    python3 proofs/coverage/merge-coverage.py \
      --kani-glob 'proofs/*/target/kani/*/kanicov_*/*kaniraw.json' \
      --census census.tsv [--allow-unmeasured walled.tsv] \
      --fuzz-lcov fuzz-float_in_diff.lcov --fuzz-lcov fuzz-float_out_diff.lcov \
      --fuzz-lcov fuzz-geo_diff.lcov --regress-lcov regress.lcov

`--census` is required (exit 2 without it). Wipe stale `kanicov_*` dirs
before a capture, or the census will report kaniraw files it did not
expect. Any harness that produced no coverage must appear in
`--allow-unmeasured` with a reason, or the merge exits 3 and writes no
summary.

## summary.json schema (contract for the viewer)

```
{
  "generated": ISO8601,
  "head_sha":  sha the numbers were measured at,
  "sloc_rule": prose statement of the denominator rule,
  "sources":   { kani|fuzz|regress: {kind, meaning, ...provenance} },
  "census":    {expected, ran, walled, failed_to_run, waived,
                kaniraw_files, kaniraw_without_census_row,
                census_closed,                    // false => DO NOT PUBLISH
                unmeasured: [{family, harness, verdict, rc, waiver}]},
  "macro_attribution": {enabled, rule, stats: {macro_body_regions,
                attributed, unresolved, lines_added,
                unresolved_functions}, index},
  "caveat":    the covered≠verified sentence (display it),
  "scope":     [crate dirs, repo-relative],
  "totals":    {sloc, kani, fuzz, regress, any,
                pct: {kani, fuzz, regress, any}},   // scope-wide
  "tree_touched": { fuzz|regress: {files_touched,
                    covered_sloc_in_touched_files,
                    sloc_of_touched_files} },        // whole tree, see below
  "crates": [ {
      name, path, sloc, kani, fuzz, regress, any,
      pct: {kani, fuzz, regress, any},
      files: [ {path, sloc, kani, fuzz, regress, any,
                detail: "files/<slug>.json"} ]
  } ]
}
```

Counts are SLOC-line counts; `any = |kani ∪ fuzz ∪ regress|`. `pct` is
`100*count/sloc` rounded to 2 places. `tree_touched` denominators are
the SLOC **of files the source touched at all** — they are NOT a
whole-tree percentage (untouched files aren't enumerated there).

Per-file detail (`files/<slug>.json`, slug = path with `/` → `__`):

```
{ "path": repo-relative file,
  "sloc":    [ascending 1-based line numbers in the denominator],
  "kani":    [covered subset], "fuzz": [...], "regress": [...] }
```

A line's display class is derived client-side: member of any/all/none of
the three arrays; lines not in `sloc` are non-code (render neutral).

## Known distortions (measured on this capture)

1. **Coverage instrumentation changes Kani solve times, sometimes
   fatally — but only for string-heavy harnesses.** `--coverage` injects
   one cover property per source region, which defeats much of the formula
   slicing win on string harnesses: e.g. `text-slice::proofs::eq_bytea_substr`
   proves in seconds in the plain per-commit tier but did not solve in
   560s under `--coverage`. 14 of 165 attempted harnesses walled this way
   (9 of text-slice's 14; jsonb's `eq_cmp_obj_obj_1_2` additionally blew
   the 6 GiB RSS watchdog at 226s vs a 22s baseline). Full run ledger:
   `proofs/coverage/coverage-runs.log`.
   **The inflation is NOT uniform.** Measured on 196 scalar-family
   harnesses (2026-07-30 smoke): **2.0x wall including one cargo build per
   family** — 914 s against 453.6 s of SUITE baseline solve time — with
   **0 of 196 walled**. True solve inflation for scalar families is well
   under 2x. The earlier "3-10x" figure was extrapolated from the string
   families and does not generalise; do not use it for budgeting.
   Harnesses that walled under coverage are listed in
   `coverage-runs.log`; their functions show as UNDER-covered relative
   to what the plain suite actually proves. No verdict changed on any
   harness that did complete (all completed harnesses reported
   VERIFICATION SUCCESSFUL, matching their ledger status).
2. **Stubbed seams count as covered via the stub.** Harnesses run with
   `-Z stubbing` (mcx allocation stubs etc.); lines reached only inside
   stubbed-out callees are attributed to the stub crate, not the real
   seam implementation — the real implementation shows uncovered.
3. **Kani region granularity** (see SLOC rule): multi-line COVERED
   regions mark every intersecting line; expect a small overcount vs
   LLVM lines.
4. **Regress run environment**: measured run scored 218/230 ok (12 files
   red on this host: create_index, select_distinct, subselect, join,
   portals, tidscan, incremental_sort, limit, partition_join,
   partition_prune, partition_aggregate, memoize) vs the fleet's
   230/230 — host-load flakes + local env (homebrew PG 18.4 seed
   cluster, no `regress.dylib` for the C-function tests). Red files
   still execute code up to their first divergence, so regress coverage
   here is a slight UNDER-estimate of the fleet suite's true footprint.
5. **Fuzz corpora are session artifacts.** The campaign corpora
   (666/106/705 inputs) live in the fuzzing worktree, not in git; a
   regeneration from `gen_seeds.sh` + a fresh campaign will differ.
6. **The instrumented server is slower** (counter increments +
   `fast-profile` codegen). Irrelevant to which lines execute, but do
   not reuse this binary for any perf number.
7. **Fleet-solved families are entirely absent from local captures.**
   A proof family whose solves happened on the fleet leaves NO kaniraw
   on this laptop, so every function it proves reads 0% here — the
   capture systematically zeroes exactly the hardest-won proofs.
   Concrete case: `proofs/float-agg` (29 runqueue harnesses over the
   float8[] aggregate transitions/finals — avg, var/stddev pop+samp,
   covar, corr, regr_*) was solved on the fleet and re-verified at main
   (`proofs/FLEET-DECODE-2026-07-30.md` §2: the three sqrt-bearing grids
   prove 3.9/5.4/16.0 s under the det-sqrt model), yet at the time of
   this capture it had **zero rows in SUITE.tsv** — a dark family — and
   zero kaniraw locally. That is why `float/src/aggregates.rs` reads
   0/225 Kani-covered in the table above despite ~29 proved-on-fleet
   harnesses over its functions. (The dark-harness sweep, commit
   `50b4e42892` on main, has since registered all 31 float-agg harnesses
   as `expected=unmeasured`; the coverage number stays 0 until a capture
   actually runs them.)
   **Rule for any full-tree capture:** enumerate expected families from
   SUITE.tsv, then either (a) include a fleet leg — run the fleet-solved
   families under `--coverage` on the fleet and merge their kaniraw plus
   census like any local family — or (b) list every one of their
   harnesses in `--allow-unmeasured` with reason `fleet-solved, no local
   coverage leg`, so the census records them UNMEASURED instead of
   letting their functions masquerade as uncovered. The fail-closed
   census makes silent omission an error only for harnesses the capture
   *expected*; a family missing from the joblist entirely is invisible
   to it, which is why the joblist must be derived from SUITE.tsv, not
   from which families happen to build locally.

## Cost of a full-tree run (if wanted)

- Regress + merge: cheap — the capture already IS whole-tree
  (instrumented build ~3 min on 14 cores, schedule run ~8 min, lcov
  export ~1 min). Extending the report grid to every crate is a
  scope-file edit; the only real cost is artifact size (~2,000 files'
  per-file detail JSONs, est. 30-60 MB).
- Fuzz: minutes per target (corpus replay); bounded by corpus size.
- Kani is the expensive axis, but **cheaper than previously estimated**.
  Measured, not extrapolated (2026-07-30 smoke, 196 harnesses over 9
  families): the inflation factor is **2.0x wall including builds** for
  scalar families, not 3-10x. The per-commit tier is 862 harnesses over
  ~40 families with a baseline solve sum of 2,059 s (0.57 h), so the
  scalar bulk is ~70 min of solve+build. The whole risk is the wall class:
  string/jsonb families walled 14 of 165 last capture; at a 900 s cap
  expect ~15-25 walls tree-wide = 4-6 h of pure timeout, which a **300 s
  cap on the string-heavy families** cuts to ~2 h.
  **Realistic total: ~2.5-4 h serial on this laptop**, or well under an
  hour with fleet parallelism by family. The previous 8-16 machine-hour
  estimate assumed the 3-10x inflation applied uniformly; the smoke test
  refutes that.
  The calibration tier remains out of reach (single cells run
  minutes-to-hours before inflation).
- Disk: **~600-800 MB per family target dir** (~5.5 GB for 9 families),
  not the 2-3 GB per family previously stated. Not a constraint.
